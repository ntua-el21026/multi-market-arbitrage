# Multi_Market_Arbitrage — Design Freeze v0.1 — Pass 2

**Status:** Accepted/Frozen after adversarial review and normative amendments  
**Scope:** Research, validation, opportunity economics, costs, replay, simulation, paper methodology, capacity, testing, and performance.

## 1. Purpose

Pass 2 defines how `Multi_Market_Arbitrage` turns validated canonical market data into defensible quantitative evidence.

The system SHALL distinguish:

\[
\Pi_{\text{theoretical}}
\rightarrow
\Pi_{\text{book-executable}}
\rightarrow
\Pi_{\text{simulated}}
\rightarrow
\Pi_{\text{paper-realized}}
\rightarrow
\Pi_{\text{live-realized}}
\]

These are different objects and SHALL never be conflated.

The research layer exists to answer:

> Is an apparent market inconsistency actually monetizable after depth, costs, latency, fill mechanics, capital lock, leg risk, and uncertainty?

## 2. Research priorities

\[
\boxed{
\text{Validity}
>
\text{Conservatism}
>
\text{Reproducibility}
>
\text{Capacity realism}
>
\text{Statistical confidence}
>
\text{Headline return}
}
\]

Research that cannot be reproduced or whose fill/cost assumptions are not explicit SHALL not be used for strategy promotion.

## 3. Frozen research decisions

| Area | Decision |
|---|---|
| Opportunity inputs | Immutable `OpportunityInputSnapshot` |
| Opportunity accounting | Layered economics; no single overloaded "profit" field |
| Depth | Full executable depth, never top-of-book only |
| Maker fills | Never assumed from touch/trade-through alone |
| Cross-venue fills | Non-atomic by default unless venue mechanism proves otherwise |
| Costs | Explicit contextual cost vector; unknown required costs fail closed |
| Rebates | Separate from fees; promotion stress case assumes zero unless contractually/empirically reliable |
| Replay | Event-driven deterministic replay from recorded events |
| Paper modes | Replay-paper and live shadow-paper are distinct |
| Latency | Explicit staged model; synthetic until measured |
| Simulation | Conservative default plus stress scenarios |
| Research splits | Chronological; no random time-series split for strategy validation |
| OOS | Once inspected for tuning, it is no longer OOS |
| Parameter search | Every attempted configuration is recorded |
| Opportunity counting | Lifecycle-based; repeated snapshots do not count as independent opportunities |
| Shared liquidity | Cannot be double-counted across simultaneous strategies/opportunities |
| Capacity | Measured as a curve, not linearly extrapolated |
| Performance | Measured with p50/p95/p99 and throughput; correctness invariants remain mandatory |

## 4. Economic layers

### 4.1 Theoretical economics

Uses validated prices/payoffs but may ignore execution depth and trading frictions.

Purpose:
- discovery;
- mathematical inconsistency measurement;
- upper-bound diagnostics.

It SHALL NOT be reported as expected realized profit.

### 4.2 Book-executable economics

For aggressive/taker-style execution only, uses:
- exact observed order-book depth;
- exact hedge ratios;
- known deterministic costs;
- precision/tick rules;
- quantity/minimum constraints;
- capital requirements.

It assumes zero decision/network latency and no adverse book change between observation and execution.

Therefore:

\[
\Pi_{\text{book-executable}}
\]

is an **analytical upper bound**, not a guarantee.

Passive maker orders do not receive `book-executable` status because fill probability is unknown.

### 4.3 Simulated economics

Uses a deterministic execution simulator with explicit:
- decision latency;
- network/venue latency;
- queue model;
- partial fills;
- cancellations;
- leg ordering;
- adverse selection;
- state changes;
- own depth consumption;
- costs.

### 4.4 Paper-realized economics

Generated by live shadow-paper or explicitly labelled replay-paper execution.

Paper P&L requires simulated fills produced by the configured fill model against subsequent observed market events.

### 4.5 Live-realized economics

Generated only from actual venue acknowledgements, fills, fees, rebates, funding, settlement, transfers, and ledger entries.

No research estimate may be relabelled as realized.

## 6. Opportunity identity and lifecycle counting

Successive detections of the same economic dislocation SHALL form one `OpportunityLifecycle`, not many independent samples.

An opportunity lifecycle records:
- first seen;
- last seen;
- state changes;
- peak gross edge;
- peak net book-executable edge;
- maximum feasible size;
- duration;
- number of observations;
- expiry/rejection reason.

Independent opportunity counts SHALL use lifecycle identity and clustering rules, not raw scanner event count.

All legs must jointly satisfy strategy-specific coherence constraints.

## 8. Full-depth sizing

For required quantity \(q\), each aggressive leg is priced by walking the actual available levels.

For a buy leg:

\[
Cost(q)=\sum_j p_j\,\Delta q_j
\]

subject to:

\[
\sum_j \Delta q_j=q
\]

and venue precision/minimum rules.

Top-of-book price alone SHALL never determine executable size.

The strategy SHALL compute a **net-edge capacity curve** rather than one size point.

Every component has:

Unknown material cost does not default to zero.

## 10. Cost certainty classes

```text
KNOWN_DETERMINISTIC
KNOWN_RULE_VARIABLE_OUTCOME
ESTIMATED
UNKNOWN
REALIZED
```

Promotion metrics SHALL distinguish deterministic costs from estimates.

Maker rebates and promotional incentives are separate positive cashflows, never silently netted into a fee number.

## 11. Replay model

Replay is event-driven and reproduces:
- recorded observation ordering;
- venue sequence semantics;
- reference data as known at time;
- market-state invalidation/resynchronization;
- opportunity detection;
- simulated order lifecycle.

Replay SHALL not interpolate invented market states between recorded events.

## 12. Simulation regimes

Required regimes:

```text
IDEALIZED_UPPER_BOUND
BASE
CONSERVATIVE
STRESS
```

`IDEALIZED_UPPER_BOUND` is diagnostic only.

Strategy promotion is driven primarily by `CONSERVATIVE`, with survival under defined `STRESS` scenarios.

Scenario assumptions are versioned configuration, not hidden constants.

## 13. Maker fill rule

A resting order SHALL NOT be considered filled merely because:
- the market touched its price;
- last trade printed at its price;
- the market later moved through its price.

Queue priority and executable flow are required.

When queue position cannot be observed exactly, the model must use conservative uncertainty bounds.

## 14. Taker fill rule

A taker order submitted at simulated time \(t_s\) sees the first valid book state available after the configured latency path.

Its fill consumes its own simulated depth.

If sufficient valid depth is absent at that time, the unfilled quantity remains unfilled or follows the explicit order policy.

The simulator may not use the earlier detection book as the guaranteed execution book.

## 15. Partial fills and residual exposure

Partial fills create explicit residual inventory.

P&L reporting SHALL separate:
- locked/realized cashflow;
- open inventory;
- liquidation value;
- mark-to-market;
- residual risk.

A partially hedged arbitrage is not reported as fully locked profit.

## 16. Cross-venue leg risk

Cross-venue execution is non-atomic unless proven otherwise.

Simulation must model:
- leg ordering;
- simultaneous-send approximation where configured;
- different latencies;
- partial fills;
- cancellation races;
- hedge failure;
- emergency unwind policy.

A theoretical payoff identity does not eliminate execution leg risk.

## 17. Adverse selection

Maker strategies SHALL report post-fill markouts at configurable horizons.

Example metric:

\[
Markout_h = s\,(P_{future,h}-P_{fill})
\]

with sign convention chosen so positive/negative interpretation is explicit.

Markout horizons are configuration/data-driven and may differ by strategy.

## 18. Capital and capacity

Required measures include:
- capital committed;
- collateral locked;
- settlement delay;
- residual exposure capital;
- venue-specific reserve;
- opportunity duration;
- capital turnover;
- feasible size at edge thresholds.

Capacity SHALL be a function:

\[
C(e,t,\text{state})
\]

rather than one scalar.

No linear extrapolation from small-size edge is permitted.

## 19. Shared-liquidity deconfliction

Two strategies may observe the same executable book liquidity.

Portfolio-level research SHALL prevent double-counting:
- the same order-book quantity;
- the same capital;
- mutually incompatible simultaneous fills.

Standalone strategy results may be reported separately, but aggregate opportunity P&L must be produced by an explicit allocator/deconfliction simulation.

## 20. Opportunity overlap

Repeated or overlapping signals are statistically dependent.

Research SHALL cluster observations by:
- opportunity lifecycle;
- market/event;
- time window;
- shared underlying;
- shared liquidity where relevant.

Raw signal count SHALL not be used as an IID sample count.

## 22. Data partitioning and leakage

Time-dependent research uses chronological splits.

Generic sequence:

```text
RESEARCH / DEVELOPMENT
        ↓
TRAIN / CALIBRATION where needed
        ↓
VALIDATION
        ↓
OOS
        ↓
PAPER
```

Random time-series splitting is prohibited for final strategy validation.

Any parameter fitted from future data creates leakage.

## 23. OOS integrity

Once OOS results influence:
- parameter selection;
- threshold selection;
- feature selection;
- strategy logic;
- simulator tuning;

that segment is no longer OOS.

A new untouched holdout is required.

Repeated OOS peeking is explicitly prohibited.

## 24. Multiple testing

Every parameter/strategy trial is recorded.

Research SHALL report:
- number of configurations evaluated;
- selection procedure;
- primary metric;
- robustness across adjacent parameters/regimes.

The system must not publish only the winning run while discarding the search history.

## 25. Missing-data bias

Periods with:
- collector downtime;
- invalid books;
- sequence gaps;
- unknown fees;
- missing reference data;

are explicitly represented.

Dropping difficult periods without reporting them is prohibited.

Strategy metrics SHALL include data-availability coverage.

## 26. Paper modes

Two different modes exist:

### Replay-paper
Orders are simulated during deterministic historical replay.

### Shadow-paper
The system runs in real time, generates order intents, records decision timestamps, and simulates fills against subsequent live market events without transmitting orders.

Shadow-paper is more representative for runtime latency and operational behavior.

The modes SHALL never be merged in reporting.

## 27. Paper P&L valuation

Paper P&L SHALL distinguish:
- realized simulated cashflows;
- open inventory;
- conservative liquidation value;
- mark-to-mid analytical value.

Promotion decisions SHALL NOT rely solely on mark-to-mid profit for residual inventory.

## 28. Statistical reporting

Research must not assume opportunity observations are IID.

Where confidence intervals are required, use methods appropriate for dependent market data, such as:
- market/event clustering;
- time-block bootstrap;
- regime stratification.

The exact statistical method is strategy-specific but must be documented.

## 30. Annualization policy

Annualized returns MAY be reported only with:
- actual holding/capital-time assumptions;
- capacity;
- opportunity frequency;
- concurrency constraints.

A single five-minute trade return SHALL never be naively compounded into a headline annual return without explicit caveats and capacity model.

Primary capital-efficiency metric:

\[
CapitalTime = \int C(t)\,dt
\]

and:

\[
ReturnOnCapitalTime=
\frac{NetPnL}{CapitalTime}
\]

with units stated explicitly.

## 31. Strategy promotion evidence

A strategy may move from research toward paper only when:
- input data quality is sufficient;
- economics are positive after known costs;
- results survive conservative simulation;
- stress behavior is acceptable;
- performance is not concentrated in one anomalous interval;
- capacity is nontrivial relative to intended capital;
- parameter behavior is robust;
- experiment/OOS integrity is clean;
- assumptions are documented.

No fixed universal sample size is imposed in Pass 2; strategy-specific minimum evidence must be defined before promotion evaluation.

## 32. Testing philosophy

Testing must include:
- unit;
- property-based;
- golden fixtures;
- deterministic replay;
- integration;
- protocol conformance;
- fuzz/adversarial;
- fault injection;
- metamorphic economics;
- performance regression.

Examples of economic metamorphic properties:
- higher fee cannot improve identical-trade net P&L;
- less available depth cannot increase executable capacity;
- worse latency cannot be assumed to improve fill quality;
- adding known cost cannot increase net edge;
- identical replay input must produce identical state/output hashes.

## 33. Performance philosophy

Performance optimizations may change implementation but not semantics.

Measure per-stage:
- throughput;
- queue depth;
- p50;
- p95;
- p99;
- max latency;
- CPU;
- memory;
- storage throughput.

Required stages include:
- receive-to-journal;
- decode;
- normalize;
- book update;
- validation;
- scanner;
- simulation;
- persistence.

No validation layer may be disabled merely to satisfy a latency target.

## 34. Pass 2 fundamental invariants

### R-01 — Layered economics
Theoretical, book-executable, simulated, paper-realized, and live-realized P&L are distinct.

### R-02 — Full-depth economics
Executable sizing walks actual available depth.

### R-03 — Maker non-assumption
Passive fills require evidence/modelled queue/flow; touch is not fill.

### R-04 — Latency-aware execution
Execution simulation uses post-latency market state.

### R-05 — Residual exposure honesty
Partial hedges are explicit open risk, not locked arbitrage.

### R-06 — Unknown cost fails closed
Material unknown cost prevents net-executable classification.

### R-07 — Rebate separation
Rebates/incentives remain separate positive cashflows with explicit certainty.

### R-08 — Lifecycle counting
Repeated observations of one dislocation are not independent opportunities.

### R-09 — Shared-liquidity deconfliction
Aggregate strategy P&L cannot spend the same liquidity/capital twice.

### R-10 — Chronological validation
Final time-series validation does not use random future leakage.

### R-11 — OOS sanctity
OOS touched for tuning ceases to be OOS.

### R-12 — Search-history retention
All evaluated parameter/configuration trials are recorded.

### R-13 — Missing-data transparency
Invalid/missing periods remain visible in coverage statistics.

### R-14 — Conservative promotion
Promotion depends on conservative/stress evidence, not idealized results.

### R-15 — Capacity realism
Profitability is evaluated as a size/capital-time function.

### R-16 — Paper mode distinction
Replay-paper and live shadow-paper results are not conflated.

### R-17 — Conservative residual valuation
Open paper inventory is not promoted using mark-to-mid alone.

### R-18 — Non-IID awareness
Signal count is not automatically statistical sample size.

### R-19 — Deterministic experiment identity
Research results identify code, data, config, simulator, and cost/fill models.

### R-20 — Performance cannot weaken correctness
Latency optimization cannot bypass required validation.

## 35. Pass 2 conclusion

A strategy is not considered attractive because:

```text
visible spread > 0
```

It must survive:

```text
validated data
    ↓
semantic proof
    ↓
full-depth economics
    ↓
current costs
    ↓
latency
    ↓
fills / queue
    ↓
partial-fill leg risk
    ↓
capital lock
    ↓
capacity
    ↓
conservative simulation
    ↓
independent validation
    ↓
shadow paper
```

Only then does the project possess evidence of an economically meaningful edge.
